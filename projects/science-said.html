Science Said…

Feminist editorial design project

Type
Case study
Format
Visual zine (7 pages)
Tools
Photoshop, InDesign
Themes
Medical bias, gender, race

Overview

Science Said… is a visual zine that explores the gap between what medicine claims to know and what patients, especially marginalized patients, actually experience.

It combines research, narrative, and graphic design to expose how pain is dismissed, minimized, or misinterpreted in medical settings.

Concept

The visual style is intentionally messy and layered to reflect the chaos and frustration of navigating healthcare systems while trying to be believed. Each spread focuses on a specific phrase or assumption used in medical culture, placing it against lived experience.

This project became both a protest and a demand to be heard.

Artist statement

Science Said… is a visual zine that explores the tension between lived experience and medical authority for those who are often ignored, dismissed, or pathologized. This project utilizes real quotes, patient testimonies, and academic readings to show how racism, fatphobia, gender bias, and cultural stigma continue to shape the way pain is “treated” in healthcare settings.

As someone who has witnessed and experienced the emotional toll of chronic illness and medical gaslighting, I wanted to make a piece that pushes the boundaries of clinical language. Each page focuses on a specific phrase or assumption that contrasts with what patients actually experience. I included quotes from texts like I’m Not Lazy, I’m Ill by Melissa Maldonado-Salcedo and How Unconscious Bias in Health Care Puts Pregnant Black Women at Higher Risk by Roni Caryn Rabin to anchor the zine in real narratives and research.

The visual style is messy, layered, and intentionally unstructured. I wanted to reflect the fact that illness doesn’t follow a structured narrative, and how marginalized people often have to work twice as hard to be heard. Through mixing collage, bold text, and symbolic imagery, Science Said… becomes both a protest and a testimony. It’s a refusal to stay silent. It’s an invitation to listen more closely to the people who’ve been told, over and over, that nothing is wrong.

Process

  • Designed each page around a medical phrase that contradicts or dismisses real patient experiences.
  • Researched and collected patient narratives, quotes, and academic sources to ground the zine in real experiences.
  • Analyzed themes around gender, race, chronic illness, and medical bias to shape the visual and conceptual direction.
  • Created multi-layered spreads in Photoshop and InDesign, experimenting with collage, texture, and typography.
  • Used layered textures, collage elements, and fragmented text to mirror the chaos and frustration of navigating medical systems.
